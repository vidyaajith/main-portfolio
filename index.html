What that looks like day to day

  • Everything waits on you. Not just the big calls, the small ones too. Your team could make the decision, but they ask first because that's safer than guessing wrong.
  • Work drops between people, not within them. Nobody messes up their own task. It's the handoff, the moment work leaves one person's hands and nobody's sure whose hands it landed in.
  • You're flying on gut feel. No dashboard, no numbers to check, so you don't find out something's wrong until a client tells you.
  • Things get agreed to, then forgotten. Someone commits to something in a meeting, the week gets busy, and it just never happens. Nobody's watching to catch it.

Service areas

Different businesses, the same break.

The operating problem is structural, so it shows up everywhere. What changes is the shape it takes and which part of the system has to be built first.

01 · Agencies

Marketing

Too many accounts, not enough process. You are the quality control.

  • One delivery standard, every account
  • Client reporting that writes itself
  • Capacity planned before the next retainer
02 · Professional services

Law firms

The case work is rigorous. The operation around it is not.

  • Intake to close, one pipeline
  • Associate workload made visible
  • SOPs for repeat matter types
03 · Practitioner led

Wellness

The practitioner is the product, so growth just means more hours.

  • Enquiry to rebooking, mapped
  • Admin off the practitioner
  • Retention tracked as a number
04 · Knowledge business

Coaching businesses

The offer works. The delivery does not repeat.

  • Program productised to repeat
  • Onboarding and follow up automated
  • Associate coaches deliver to standard
05 · Project based

Construction and real estate

Sites, subcontractors and timelines moving at once, tracked by phone call.

  • Every phase owned and tracked
  • Subcontractors on a documented rhythm
  • Budget and schedule variance, weekly
06 · Recurring revenue

SaaS

Support, onboarding and churn firefighting all route through the founder.

  • Onboarding and support on a repeatable system
  • Feature requests triaged, not lost in Slack
  • Churn signals caught before renewal
